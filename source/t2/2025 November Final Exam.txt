Pure Aura (Pty) Ltd (‘Pure Aura’ or ‘the company’) is a company which manufactures
beauty and skincare products. The company was founded by Phila Bhengu in 2010,
together with his sisters, Linda and Grace Bhengu. Each of the three siblings holds an
equal shareholding in the company. The company is a small business corporation as
defined, is registered as a Category B VAT vendor and has a December financial year-
end.
1. Lease Agreement
In 2024, Pure Aura required additional funding to support its expansion plans. In order to
free up cash resources, the company disposed of its factory building and subsequently
entered into a lease agreement with the Western Cape Provincial Government for the use
of property that would serve as its new factory premises. The building to be leased was
not as big as the factory building that Pure Aura previously owned. Pure Aura required
more space in the factory, to ensure that the production levels would not decrease
because of the new space. As part of the negotiations with the Western Cape Provincial
Government, Pure Aura requested that the lease agreement include a clause requiring
the company to undertake and finance expansions to the building to create the desired
space to suit the company’s production plans. The terms of the lease agreement were as
follows:

2. Distribution Division
Pure Aura has a distribution division that is responsible for delivering trading stock to
clients once orders are completed. The division operates a fleet of delivery trucks, all of
which are owned by the company. These delivery trucks can only accommodate the driver
and one passenger as they are mainly meant to carry goods. On 31 March 2025, one of
the trucks was hijacked as it was returning to the warehouse after delivering trading stock
to clients. The delivery truck was acquired for R744 000 on 1 January 2021. The open
market value of the truck was R552 000 on the day of the hijacking incident. The truck
was not recovered. However, it was insured, and the company lodged a claim with its
insurer, who paid out an amount of R650 000. On 30 June 2025, the same date the
insurance proceeds were received, Pure Aura purchased a new delivery truck for
R1 150 000, financed partly from the insurance payout. The new delivery truck was
immediately brought into use.
Fortunately, neither of the employees in the truck was physically harmed during the
incident. They were encouraged to talk to the company psychologist to deal with the
traumatic event and the impact it may have had on them.
3. Pure Aura – Other Transactions
The tax accountant at Pure Aura was in the process of finalising the company’s normal
tax computation for the 2025 year of assessment when his wife gave birth earlier than the
expected due date. As a result, he took paternity leave before completing the tax

booked off work for three months following his surgery. Although he did not make

4. Agreement with Content Creators
Pure Aura has observed a growing trend among companies in the beauty industry where
the companies partner up with social media content creators. The management of Pure
Aura considered adopting a similar approach by gifting trading stock to selected
influencers. The company selected 25 creators, and each creator was gifted a hamper
with products with an open market value of R9 000. There was no expectation for the
creators to post about the products on their social media platforms. The products in each
hamper had cost the company R6 500. A concern was raised regarding the potential
unintended tax consequences of this arrangement. The tax accountant was already on
paternity leave when this transaction was concluded and was therefore not available to
address management’s concerns.
5. New Product Launch
In October 2025, Pure Aura launched a new skincare product as part of its premium
beauty range. Shortly after the product’s release, a customer experienced a severe
allergic reaction after using it and was hospitalised for several days. The customer
reported the incident to Pure Aura on 30 November 2025, prompting the company to seek
legal advice. Following consultation with its legal advisors, Pure Aura agreed to settle the
matter out of court and paid the customer an amount of R150 000 in compensation on 15
