Suggested Solution - Question 1
1.1
Damages and compensation
There is no specific deduction for damages and compensation therefore we would look to s11(a) read together with s23(g).
The onus is on the taxpapyer, CT Glass, to prove the amount is deductible.
The issue is whether the amount paid is in the production of income.
An amount is incurred in the production of income if it is an inevitable concomitant of the taxpayer's income-earning activities or we ask "What
action gave rise to the expenditure? Is this action so closely connected with the income earning activities of the taxpayer"
The taxpayers income earning operations is the manufacturing of alimium products, it is highly likely that an accident of this nature could happen in
this trade OR the action that gave rise to the expenditure is the employee being injured while on the job, this action is closely connected with the
income earning operations of manufacturing of the products.
Therefore the amount is in the production of income and deductible under s11(a).
Legal expenses
There is a specific deduction - s11(c) that deals with legal expenses actually incurred on any claim, dispute or action at law occuring as a result of or
due to the ordinary operations of the taxpyer in the carrying on of his trade.
The deduction under s11(c) follows the nature of the underlying claim and is therefore not capital in nature.
The deduction for the compensation is allowed under s11(a) therefore the legal fees paid to the lawyer will be allowed under s11(c).

1.2 Trading stock
Supply
There is a supply in terms of section 7(1)(b) of the VAT Act as goods have been imported into South Africa.
Value of supply
As the import is not from a BLNS country, the value of the supply in respect of goods imported is the customs duty value plus the customs duty
levied plus 10% of the customs duty value.
Therefore the value of the supply is 1 330 000 + (10% x 1 330 000) + 240 000 = R1 703 000
And the imposition of VAT is 15% of this amount, which is R255 450
Time of supply
The time of supply is 30 November 2020 when it was released for home consumption.
Input tax
As CT Glass is going to use the goods to make taxable supplies, an input tax deduction claim be claimed equivalent to the VAT paid on importation.
Vehicle
CT glass purchased the vehicle for use to make taxable supplies and was charged output tax by the supplier.
The double-cab meets the definition of a motor car, and therefore input denied. CT Glass is therefore denied an input tax deduction.
The insurance proceeds were received as compensation for the bakkie which was input denied, therefore as it relates to a total reinstatement of
goods where input tax was originally denied, there will be no output tax.
Building
The building was purchased from a non-vendor but it is second hand therefore the second hand goods rule applies
The value of supply is the lower of consideration and open market value
15/115 x 1 700 000 = R221 739
As this is fixed property but the second hand goods rule applies, the time of supply is to the extent of payment, deferred to registration
Therefore (1 750 000/2) x 15/115 = R114 130 can be claimed on 1 July 2020.
The balance (221 739 - 114 130)= R107 611 can be claimed on 1 August 2020.

1.5 CT Glass
Even though the payment to the government employee, would meet the requirements of s11(a), it is considered a bribe.
There is a prohibited deduction in terms of s23(o) that prevents the deduction of any payments resulting from a corrupt activity. The bribe is a
corrupt activity and therefore no deduction allowed.
Government employee
There is a special inclusion gross income paragraph (c) that deals with services rendered.
The employee received the payment in return for making sure that CT Glass wins the tender, therefore there is a causal link.
In determining whether an amount is income it is irrelevant that it was derived from an illegal or immoral activity.
The employee intended to receive this money for their own benefit and therefore it will be included in their gross income.
Ethical considerations
Payment of a bribe cannot be considered to be ethical behaviour as it is not 'right, proper and just' behaviour
Paying a bribe is a criminal offence, the employee should not accept bribes, this is unethical and illegal.
Corruption is a huge problem in South Africa and the directors of CT Glass should know better than to add to the corruption issue.
This is unethical leadership in the company as the directors are showing that they would do anything to win the tender.
The directors should have considered whether the payment of the bribe would have been 'good for themselves' and 'good for others'
There could have been other businesses who were more deserving of the tender and now won't get an opportunity
As the directors are Chartered accountants, the CPC would be applicable to them. The directors are contravening integrity and professional behaviour
