QUESTION 1  |  (56 MARKS; 84 MINUTES)
Stirology (Pty) Ltd (‘Stirology’ or ‘the company’) is a family-owned South African company that offers
hands-on, interactive, and fun cooking classes with a bit of a difference. Participants are put into
teams, and each team member is given a portion of the meal to prepare. The menu is revealed and
issued to participants on the day and is largely determined by the availability of fresh, locally
produced ingredients. Recipes, tools and guidance are also provided as needed but participants are
always encouraged to improvise. Once the meal has been cooked, all participants then gather around
the table to enjoy the meal they have prepared together.
Jade Quin and her parents, John and Jenny Quin are the trio that founded Stirology in October 2008.
Jade has remained very involved in the company’s operations since its incorporation; however, her
parents have recently taken more of a backseat. On incorporation of the company, 10 000 equity
shares were issued to Jade, John and Jenny for R5 each. There have been no transfers of these shares
or issues of new shares since incorporation of the company. Jade does not hold any other equity
shares. However, John and Jenny Quin have invested most of their pension in South African
companies that are listed on the JSE.
Stirology has a 31 October financial year-end and is a registered category A VAT vendor. The company
has requested your assistance regarding various matters concerning its tax affairs in its current year of
assessment.
Stirology had correctly calculated gross income of R4 500 000 and tax-deductible expenditure of
R850 000 before taking into account the below information:
All amounts are inclusive of VAT where appropriate unless stated otherwise or shown by the
context.

2. Customer Settlement
Jade generally requires that all customers who book with Stirology, provide her with a complete list
of all dietary requirements and allergies of all participants that are included in the booking. On receipt
of this list, she generally adapts existing recipe’s and ingredients purchased to suit the customers’
requirements. Jade also makes sure to check the ingredient lists on the packaging of all items to be
used and consumed by each group of participants to confirm that there are not traces of any specified
allergens.
On 20 March 2025, Jade received an email from a Riley Boysen, a customer that had attended a
cooking class earlier that week, to notify her that she had had a very bad allergic reaction that
required her to seek medical attention, soon after attending class. While Riley was unsure of the
exact dish or ingredient that had caused her allergic reaction, she was of the belief that she had
consumed some type of shellfish while at Stirology.
After referring back to her emails, Jade was able to confirm that Riley had in fact specified this allergy
when booking for the class, and although the menu issued to participants on the day that Jade
attended the class did not seem to include any shellfish, on further investigation of the individual
ingredients, Jade noted that the green curry paste that was used and consumed by participants
contained traces of dried shrimp. Jade had unfortunately forgotten to check the ingredients listed on the
packaging of all items used as Stirology happened to be short staffed on that particular day and Jade
had been rushing to get things ready in time for the class.
After realizing that she had made such a careless error, Jade replied to Riley’s email with a sincere
apology on 24 March 2025. In her email, she also stated that Stirology had just paid an amount of
R3 000 into Riley’s bank account to cover her medical costs. She also offered to allow Riley and ten
friends to attend a Stirology cooking class at a future date of their choosing, provided that the
--- page 3 ---
booking is made within a period of 12 months. Riley graciously accepted Jades offer on the same day,
but had not made a booking to attend the class by 31 October 2025.
Customers are generally charged a fee of R500 per person when attending a Stirology class, however,
the actual costs incurred by Stirology in providing the class amounts to a mere R100 per person.
3. Employee Benefits
In addition to Jade, Stirology employs four other full-time employees who are not connected persons to
each other or the company. The company pays all of its employees a basic salary in addition to the
additional benefits outlined below:
• The latest cooking class that is offered by Stirology begins at 6pm and is expected to end well
before 10pm. However, on some occasions, participants take longer than expected to cook
the meal which can cause the event to run overtime. All Stirology employees that are on duty
are then required to stay even later and clean up the premises after the event.
In these instances, employees that are reliant on public transport or lift clubs are generally
unable to get home. As a result, the company pays for these employees to stay in backpacking
accommodation1 in Cape Town when cooking classes run past 10pm. During the 2025 year of
assessment, the company incurred accommodation costs of R2 500 (excluding VAT) each for
two of its employees. Neither of these two employees have stayed in the backpacking
accommodation for more than one night at a time.
• Jona Lee, the company booking and operations manager, was awarded an iPad Pro as a long
service award on 31 October 2025. Jona has been employed in this position since Stirology
was first incorporated. The iPad was purchased new for R15 000 at a ‘Christmas in July Special’
on 31 July 2025. This was at a 10% discount to the usual selling price.

The company owns the following assets:
Business Premises
Stirology operates from its business premises, a free-standing house located in Woodstock, Cape
Town.
In June 2024, the company purchased this two-bedroom house for R1 800 000 when the market value
thereof amounted to R1 500 000. The premises was purchased from Thabo Tinto, who had previously

Stirology received a statement at the end of each month from the Cape Town Municipality detailing

attributable to the property rates.
Cooking Equipment
Prior to the move to the new business premises, the company did not own its own cooking equipment
and merely used what was available in the Quin family home. On 1 October 2024, a small business
loan amounting to R100 000 was taken out to cover the costs of equipping the new premises with
the necessary cooking equipment and utensils, some of which have been outlined below (e.g. The Ice
Cream Machine). The loan attracted interest at a rate of 7% per annum. No capital repayments had
been made in respect of this loan by the end of the 2025 year of assessment, while all interest
payments had been made on a monthly basis as and when due.
R20 000 of the loan amount had not been used by 31 March 2025. This excess amount was used to
purchase more equity shares in Orange Inc, a company resident in the United States of America, after
which, Stirology held 0.5% of the equity shares in the company. The company received dividends
amounting to a rand equivalent of R588 000 into its bank account, after dividends withholdings tax
was withheld at a rate of 30% in the USA.
Ice Cream Machine
On 15 October 2024, the company imported a new SnoMaster ice cream machine from a UK supplier
and non-VAT vendor for a rand equivalent purchase price of R8 200. The machine arrived in Cape
Town on 30 October 2024. Import duties of R600 were paid by Stirology, as well as VAT, and the
machine was released by SARS customs and excise on 20 November 2024.
Immediately after the machine had been delivered, Jade attended a short online training session that
was offered by the UK supplier at an additional cost of R500. The machine was brought into use
immediately thereafter on 30 November 2024.
Portable Pizza Oven
Mr Quin donated a portable Pizza oven to Stirology on 1 October 2025. He had initially purchased
the pizza oven new for an amount of R5 500 (which was equivalent to its market value) in June
2024 for home use but had not yet taken it out of the packaging. The pizza oven had a market value
of R5 000 on the date of donation.
