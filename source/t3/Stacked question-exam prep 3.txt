STACKED  |  (66 MARKS, 99 MINUTES)
Stacked (Pty) Ltd (‘Stacked’ or ‘the company’) is Cape Town’s first authentic café and
crêperie1. It offers the most soft, buttery and delicious pancakes that are infinitely versatile as
a sweet or savory option; for breakfast, lunch, dinner or dessert. Stacked is a registered
Category C VAT Vendor and only produces taxable supplies. The company has a 30
September financial year-end.
Stacked is wholly owned by French born, Ella Rose (‘Ella’), who has lived in Cape Town for
over 20 years. She has been a tax resident of South Africa since her initial move. Ella is a
former international model who has channeled her tasteful style into this French styled
crêperie that she also manages.
Ella goes through very rigorous recruitment processes in the hiring of all staff. This process
ensures that all staff are well suited for the café and have the necessary interpersonal skills
and commitment required of the hospitality industry. All employees are well compensated in
return for their services rendered with personalised salary structures depending on their
specific needs and services rendered.
All amounts exclude VAT, unless stated otherwise or shown by the context.
Waiters
Stacked’s waiting staff consists of 30 employees who each work 6 hour shifts, 5 days a week.
Waiters receive a standard R20 for each hour worked. Waiters also receive generous cash
tips directly from customers that often make up most of their take home pay.
On a monthly basis, in terms of Stacked’s general company policy, an employee of the month
is selected from their waiting staff. On 31 October 2024, Steward Watson (‘Steward’) was
selected as the employee of the month. As the winning employee, he was offered one of
Stacked’s pancake gift set hampers, consisting of pancake mix, and a range of syrups, sauces
and other pancake toppings. The hampers were regularly sold to Stacked’s customers for
R450 which is the open market value. The cost to the company is R250 (including VAT) to
prepare and assemble.
Katherine Kaley
Katherine Kaley (‘Katherine’) is a 47-year old South African resident and is the head chef at
Stacked. Katherine was employed when Stacked first opened its doors and has worked her
way up to her current position.
Katherine is in a committed long-term relationship with Olivia Battle, her best friend and
partner. Katherine and Olivia live together in their home in Zonnebloem, Cape Town. Katherine
and Olivia had bought their home for R1 800 000 in June 2012, which they own together in
equal share. The couple has no children.
Katherine earns a salary of R45 000 per month, of which she contributes 12% to the
company’s provident fund. She also makes contributions to a retirement fund annuity (‘RAF’)
of R500 per month. Katherine does not provide any proof of the RAF contributions to Stacked.
In addition, Katherine received a performance-linked bonus of R80 000 on 31 October 2024
which was approved by the management of Stacked at the company’s 2024 financial year-
end.

Other information relating to Katherine’s income and expenditure for the 2025 year of
assessment is outlined below:
1. During October 2024, Katherine was required to attend a five-day training course in
Paris, France. Katherine and her partner decided to make a holiday of this trip and so
extended it by another 10 days. They both flew directly to Paris on an Emirates flight,
which cost Stacked R13 500 for each of them. Due to the delay in booking the flights,
Stacked paid a premium of 20% on the price charged for the cheapest seat on the
same plane. In addition to the cost of the flight, stacked made the following payments:
• R10 000 for five nights of hotel accommodation for Katherine only;
• An additional allowance of R1 000 per day for five days to cover meals and
other incidental costs.
Katherine and her partner had to pay all remaining expenses out of Katherine’s
allowance. They stayed in a B & B for the remaining 10 days at a cost of R1 200 per
day and they incurred an average of R800 per day each on meals, drinks and other
incidental costs while in Paris2.
2. Every second year, Katherine receives an allowance of R1 500 to purchase a
traditional chef’s uniform that she is required to wear while at work. She does not have
much flexibility as to the design of the uniform, as the uniform must consist of a hat, a
double-breasted jacket, pants and an apron, all of which are sold together in one
package. Katherine is however given the option of purchasing a black or white uniform
in a fit that suits her body type. She purchases standard iron-on Stacked logos
separately and irons them onto the individual pieces. Katherine received her most
recent uniform allowance on 1 June 2024.
3. Katherine had purchased an annuity, at a cost of R650 000, on 1 October 2016 from
RCS Investments (Pty) Ltd. In terms of the purchase agreement, Katherine was
entitled to a monthly receipt of R12 000 over a 10-year period from the end of the
month in which she had purchased the annuity. On 1 October 2024, Katherine
commuted this annuity to free up cash for her holiday to France. She received R180
000 on commutation.
Olivia.Battle
Olivia Battle (‘Olivia’) was one of the founders and the CEO of Tutor Me 2 (Pty) Ltd (‘Tutor Me
2’), a South African based company. On 31 December 2024, Olivia retired after reaching the
company’s mandatory retirement age of 55. When Olivia and her sister incorporated the
company, during 2005, they each purchased 50 000 equity shares at R1 each.
In terms of Olivia’s employment package,
• Tutor Me 2 will continue to pay the monthly medical aid scheme contributions for Olivia,
her partner and one other dependent of her choosing after her retirement.
• Furthermore, in the event of Olivia’s death, the company will continue to pay these
contributions, on behalf of the living dependents.
The medical aid contributions for the 2025-year of assessment amounted to R3 500 per month
for the main member and an additional R1 300 for each additional dependent.

As her mother’s primary caregiver, Olivia had selected her mother, Muriel, as the additional
dependent on her medical aid. Muriel has been deaf since birth and had recently found out
that she was a candidate for a cochlear implant. In June 2024, she underwent surgery for this,
for which Olivia received an invoice addressed to her for qualifying medical expenses for an
amount of R350 000. This amount was not recoverable from the medial aid scheme. By the
end of the 2025 year of assessment, Olivia had only paid half of the amount due.
Sadly, Olivia passed away on 31 January 2025 from an unexpected and severe allergic
reaction to a bee sting. Olivia’s last will and testament provided as follows:
• The interest in her home in Zonnebloem was to be bequeathed to Katherine. The
market value of the full property was R3,75 million on the date of Olivia’s death.
• The proceeds of a life insurance policy of R2 million were to go to Katherine. Olivia
and Katherine had made contributions of R300 000 (inclusive of interest at 6%) each
towards this policy.
• Olivia’s shares in Tutor Me 2 were bequeathed to her sister, Sammy, who was also
the co-founder of the company. Olivia’s shares had a market value of R580 000 on the
date of death and R590 000 on the date that Olivia’s estate was wound up.
• A tax free saving account with a market value of R1 340 000 was bequeathed to Muriel.
Olivia has made contributions to this account of R450 000 during her lifetime.
• Olivia’s car was left to her niece, Kylie. The car had a market value of
R250 000 on the date of Olivia’s death but was sold by Kylie for R200 000 soon
thereafter. Olivia had originally purchased the car for R400 000 five years prior to her
death.
Olivia’s taxable income before taking the above information into account was R1,25 million.
