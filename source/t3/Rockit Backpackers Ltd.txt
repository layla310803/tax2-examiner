Rockit Backpackers  |  (41 MARKS, 62 MINUTES)
Rockit Backpackers Ltd (‘Rockit’) is a category C Value-Added Tax vendor, and carries on
the business of selling amateur and professional backpacks and climbing equipment. Rockit
also recently won ‘Adventure Consultant of the Year’ for its work in providing top athletes
with preparation consultation before they embark on technical mountain climbing journeys. In
addition the company also provides transportation services to various hiking groups and
climbers to the various adventure hotspots of the Western Cape area. Rockit provides this
service with its company-owned mini-bus.
Rockit is currently preparing its one-month VAT 201 return for the period ended 31 October
2024, which is also its year-end. The company’s financial manager was recently invited to
join an executive team on a once-in-a-lifetime trip to the base camp of Everest and was
therefore unable to complete the VAT 201 return. As a result, Rockit has asked you assist
with the preparation of the VAT 201 return and to assist them with certain VAT issues they
are unfamiliar with.
All amounts include VAT where appropriate, unless stated otherwise. Where applicable, VAT
invoices are on hand.

2. Purchase of Assets

3. Administration expenses
During October 2024, Rockit incurred administration expenses of R284 000. These
expenses included stationery, paper and Wifi charges, amongst others, and were
used by administrative staff working in the hiking and athlete consulting division only.
4. Vehicle purchases
October was considered a month for capital investment. Rockit purchased two
vehicles. One was a double-cab bakkie, which was used as a delivery vehicle for
sales of its backpacks and climbing equipment. The second was a new 18-seater
Volkswagen mini-bus kombi, which was used with in the transportation offering
provided by Rockit. The two vehicles cost R640 000 and R750 000 respectively.
5. Rockit sold a computer to L-Way (Pty) Ltd (‘L-Way’) for R16 000. L-Way is a
backpack manufacturer and manufactures many of the products sold by Rockit. The
computer will be used by L-Way to conduct all the administration activities of its
business. Rockit had used the computer for the administration of product sales
exclusively. The market value on 20 October 2024, the date of sale and on which
payment was received, was R18 000.
6. Salaries and wages of R373 000 were paid to employees for the month of October.
7. Rockit Ltd purchased leather tags from a non-vendor for R1 500. These tags were
actually off-cuts from the non-vendor’s trade and were considered used. The open
market value on the day the tags were bought was R1 000. Rockit, in turn, sold the
tags to Cape Union Tart (Pty) Ltd (‘CUT’). CUT will use the leather in its
manufacturing process. The selling price was R2 000, when the open market value
was R2 250. The transaction in respect of the purchase and sale of these tags took
place on October 2024.
8. One of the new assistant accountants at Rockit had completely forgotten his tax
knowledge that he had acquired whilst at University and wanted to clarify the
following point with you regarding the November 2024 VAT period:
“I was just wondering how I would deal with the following from a VAT perspective. I
am busy calculating the sales for the month of November for both consulting as well
as the transportation offerings of our company. It seems like there was a large
volume of credit sales for this month and most of them were with non-VAT vendors.
Surely Rockit would not need to incur Output VAT on these sales as they have not
been paid and the other party claimed no input. The value of sales on credit for
consultancy and transport was R1 100 000 and R385 000 respectively before making
any VAT adjustments. Please would you consider clearing all this up. All this coffee
making has made my brain fuzzy.”
